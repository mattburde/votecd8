Methodology & Privacy

Ballot Journey — CD-8 neighborhood ballot-return game

🧪 This is a demo-mode prototype. Everything you do here is stored only in your own browser's local storage. It is not shared with other devices, not visible to anyone else, and is not a real multi-person leaderboard yet. See "What demo mode means" below.

What this is

A turnout game: it helps you prepare for your mail ballot before it arrives, track it, find a drop box, and — if you choose to share a confirmation screenshot — celebrate when it's counted, together with a neighborhood/team you pick.

This is not a persuasion product. It never asks how you voted, never recommends a candidate, and never tries to infer who you're voting for.

Game data vs. official election data

Drop-box locations shown here come directly from this project's verified, official Adams County data (the same data behind the official drop-box map) — never re-typed or re-derived.

Everything else — your status, your neighborhood's confirmed count, badges, the leaderboard — is game data: self-reported or submitted by participants, reviewed by a human, and never presented as an official vote count or official turnout figure.

"Game-confirmed ballots" is not "official ballots cast." The leaderboard counts people who played this game and had a submission approved here — a small, self-selected group, not a measurement of your neighborhood's actual turnout.

What demo mode means

This repository is a static website with no server and no database. This prototype was built without credentials to provision one, so it ships with a documented, swappable storage interface (game/data-adapter.js) behind a local, single-device implementation. That means, right now:

  • Your data lives only in this browser. Switching devices starts fresh.
  • The leaderboard only reflects neighborhoods/participants created in this browser.
  • The admin dashboard can only see what's in the same browser (useful for testing the flow end-to-end on one device, not for a real multi-person pilot).

A real pilot with other people needs a real backend implementing the same interface. See the project's deliverables doc for the concrete recommendation.

Screenshot privacy

If you submit a BallotTrax confirmation screenshot:

  • It is read only inside your own browser, to compute a one-way "fingerprint" (a short hash) used to spot likely duplicate submissions.
  • The image itself is never uploaded, never sent anywhere, and never stored — not even in this browser's local storage. Once the fingerprint is computed, the image is discarded.
  • Only the fingerprint, your attestation ("this shows my ballot was counted"), and a timestamp are kept.
  • Your name, email, or any other BallotTrax account detail is never extracted, stored, or displayed.
  • Nothing about your submission is ever shown publicly or to other participants.
A real deployment that wants a human to actually look at a screenshot before approving it would need to briefly and securely store the image for that one review, then delete it — this prototype doesn't do that, so review currently relies on your attestation and the duplicate-fingerprint signal alone. That tradeoff is intentional for this prototype and documented as a known limitation, not hidden.

Duplicate & abuse protection

Submitting the same (or a near-identical) screenshot twice is flagged automatically by comparing fingerprints, and routed to manual review rather than silently accepted or silently blocked. This is lightweight, reasonable abuse resistance for a civic game — not identity verification, and not a claim that it's foolproof.

Neighborhoods

For this first prototype, a "neighborhood" is a name you choose — not a government boundary, not inferred from your address, and not linked to any demographic or political data. This avoids inferring anything about who lives where. A future version could roll these up into county or precinct totals for broader comparisons; that hasn't been built yet.

Questions or concerns

This is an independent civic project, not an official government tool. See the outreach analysis page for a similar disclaimer style, and the project's game/DELIVERABLES.md for the full list of legal and privacy items still awaiting human review before any real-world pilot.
